## 1. Authority — what this document inherits and does not redefine

The aesthetic authority is `.claude/agents/industrial-design-cmf.md`, with the family direction already expressed for aquarium hardware in `products/aquarium/lighting/PLATFORM.md` §10. Held precisely:

- **Target reaction:** "They cared about every detail. This feels like a premium, thoughtfully engineered product." Premium by evidence of care, **not** by luxury signalling.
- **Avoid:** executive-office luxury, minimalism for its own sake, jewellery-like products, glossy prestige electronics, generic Apple imitation, unnecessary gold/chrome accents, luxury-signalling ornament, products designed primarily to look expensive, and any silhouette a hobbyist would mistake for Chihiros, ONF, ADA, Twinstar or Week Aqua.
- **Character:** highly considered, functional, tactile, detail-rich, visually distinctive, contemporary, maker/engineering-aware, photographable, honest about materials and construction; seen close up, alongside plants, wood, stone and glass, and handled during maintenance.
- **Family rules from the lights (dry side):** function is the decoration; quiet above the tank, rewarding in the hand; matte bead-blasted graphite, not pure black and not natural silver; few honest dark fasteners; one small laser-marked wordmark; kraft/board packaging with cavities, no foam, "a well-organised tool case, not a gift box"; photographability including reflections; wear that looks intentional (Leatherman/Knipex).
- **Owner taste** (context): tactile, character-driven identities; names grounded in real trade terminology rather than invented decorative language.

What this document adds for lily pipes is a way to **evaluate water-side hardware** against that direction, a reading of the market, and **opportunities and questions**. It does not choose a form, a finish or a recognition cue.

## 2. Evaluation criteria

The brief translates to four principles for parts that live in water:

1. **Quiet at viewing distance** — the hardware does not compete with plants, wood and stone ("quiet in the tank").
2. **Rewarding at maintenance distance** — handled every 2–6 weeks; care should be obvious in the hand.
3. **Function is the decoration** — every visible feature (slot, flare, clip, bead, joint) does a job and looks like it does.
4. **Honest ageing** — aquarium hardware ages fast (biofilm, scale, tea staining); a Trophic part ages in a way that looks intentional or can be restored, never like failure.

| # | Criterion | Judged | Viewing condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Proportion | Diameter vs length vs termination; inflow/outflow sized for each other; holds across tank sizes | Front glass, 1–2 m |
| C2 | Silhouette and section | Clarity of profile; distinct without mimicking known forms | Front, top |
| C3 | Visual weight in water | Refraction, LED glints, apparent thickness, top view with lid off | Under lights |
| C4 | Material and finish | Honest material, chosen gloss, consistency; relation to graphite light family | Hand, 30 cm |
| C5 | Bends and transitions | No ovality/wrinkles; joints, swages and terminations resolved and aligned | Hand; front |
| C6 | Mounting hardware | Parts count, visibility, meeting the rimless edge, material/colour match | Top, rim |
| C7 | Hose interface | Rim crossing, hose colour and ageing, barb/collar transition, clamps | Top, rear |
| C8 | Inflow/outflow consistency | One family including skimmer and accessories | Whole tank |
| C9 | Scape complementarity | Bright iwagumi, dark jungle, nature style; white/black backgrounds | Whole tank |
| C10 | Appearance under real use | Fingerprints, limescale, biofilm/algae (inside glass vs outside steel), tea staining, scratches, clip wear; restorability | 1, 3, 12 months |
| C11 | Maintenance tactility | Removal, grip, obvious disassembly, every wetted surface brushable, no fragility anxiety | Wet hands |
| C12 | Photographability | Glints and front-glass reflections, macro shots, contest-photo tolerance | Camera, lights on |

**Scoring:** 1–5 per criterion; **3 = competent category norm**. Weights: C3, C10, C12 ×1.5; C8, C9 ×0.75; others ×1.0; maximum 65. **Veto:** any criterion scoring 1, or C2 = 1 through competitor mimicry, rules a direction out regardless of total. Score twice — new/clean and after ~3 months of normal maintenance; the gap is itself a finding. Full anchor text for every score level is in Appendix A; key anchors: C3-5 "present only when looked for, and quiet does not depend on perfect cleanliness"; C6-5 "mounting elegance: minimal contact, obvious how it works, a detail worth photographing"; C10-5 "wear looks intentional; after a year it looks used-and-cared-for, not tired"; C11-5 "confident weight, clear disassembly cues, every internal surface reachable, parts that feel like tools".

### 3.1 How appearance changes in real use (the criterion most often ignored)

| Material/finish | Fingerprints | Mineral deposits | Algae/biofilm | Cleaning wear | Net ageing character |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clear glass | Invisible in water | White scale at waterline, visible | **Visible inside, fast** (cleaning once or twice a month recommended by Aquasabi) | None, but breakage risk each clean | Beautiful clean, obviously dirty within weeks |
| Clear PETG/acrylic | Invisible | Visible | Visible inside | **Scratches/haze from brushes** | Degrades permanently |
| Natural polished stainless | Visible above water | Visible spots | Hidden inside; outside film shows as dulling | Polishes; scratches show on mirror finish | Durable; glints; can look "industrial" |
| Bead-blasted stainless | Hidden | Less visible *[inferred]* | Roughness may hold biofilm — **flag** | Even burnishing of high points *[inferred]* | Potentially honest ageing, pending hygiene test |
| Dark PVD/coating on stainless | Hidden | Visible as light spots on dark | Hidden | **Chips at clip/bend expose bright steel** *[inferred]* | Recedes best new; worst if coating fails |
| Stock plastic | Hidden | Hidden | Hidden (green hides green) | Scratches irrelevant | Tolerable, no intent |

## 4. Opportunities for a Trophic water-side design language

Directions and questions, **not forms**. Owners of flagged conflicts: **Mfg** manufacturing-sourcing-engineer · **QA** qa-reliability-engineer · **Sys** systems-architect (hydraulics, filter interface) · **Bio** plant-science-specialist (livestock, CO₂/surface).

| # | Opportunity (direction) | Trade-off / conflict flagged | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| O1 | **Compete on considered presence, not invisibility.** A durable part will be seen; make it quiet, low-glint and detail-rich | Gives up day-1 invisibility and contest-photo buyers; should win at month 6–12. Size of each buyer group unknown | Design + interviews |
| O2 | **Finish related to the graphite family, chosen for water.** Candidate families to explore: bead-blasted natural stainless; dark PVD over textured stainless; chemical/thermal blackening; textured electropolish; a non-metal where it earns its place | Blast roughness may hold biofilm and reduce passivation; dark PVD may chip at clip/bend; blackening chemistry may be unsafe for shrimp; grade and heat tint drive tea staining; electropolish can drift to mirror (glints, jewellery look) | QA, Bio, Mfg |
| O3 | **Design wear to look intentional and restorable** — brushing should burnish evenly, not chip | Through-material finishes age honestly but are lighter and more reflective; dark coatings recede but may age badly. **The central CMF tension of the product** | QA owns appearance acceptance |
| O4 | **Make serviceability the visible detail** — removable intake tip, outlet joint, hose collar as the family's characteristic details; obvious, satisfying, cannot be refitted wrongly | Every joint is a biofilm crevice and potential shrimp trap; return-side joints must not leak, loosen or re-aim; intake slot geometry is set by livestock safety and clogging, styled only afterwards | QA, Bio, Sys, Mfg |
| O5 | **Mounting as a signature.** The market's weakest criterion (clear suction cups, clear acrylic holders). A dark, few-part, rimless-glass-aware clip designed with the pipe, reusable across future wet accessories | Clamp force and contact material on glass edges (safety); rimmed tanks and a 4–15 mm glass range; **dark black-oxide fasteners from the lights will corrode in water — the dry-side fastener rule may not transfer; "no fasteners in water" may be needed** | QA, Sys, Mfg |
| O6 | **Resolve the rim crossing** — pipe, collar and hose read as one line; hose tone chosen with the pipe; consider how it looks once clear hose clouds | Retention under filter pressure with standard 12/16 and 16/22 hose; dark hose hides blockages; plasticiser safety | Sys, Mfg, QA, Bio |
| O7 | **Termination distinctive by function, not ornament** — derived from the flow job, reads as a tool nozzle; no petal or bloom metaphors | Flare angle and exit area set velocity, ripple, CO₂ loss; proportions follow the hydraulic target, not the reverse; adjustable outlets add joints | Sys, Bio |
| O8 | **A calm skimmer** that shares the intake language with a legible float action | Level range, float reliability, entrapment guard and float material come first | Sys, QA, Bio, Mfg |
| O9 | **Section, bend-radius rule or a small index/grip feature** as a quiet cue that reads in the hand | Non-round sections are harder to bend, brush and may raise losses; prefer a cue on the collar rather than the flow path | Mfg, Sys, QA |
| O10 | **One small mark where it is seen during servicing**, never front-facing in water; packaging that communicates serviceability (pipes, clips, brush each with a place; no foam) | Laser marking can damage the passive layer on submerged stainless — mark above water or re-passivate; foam-free protection of long parts must be proven | QA, Mfg |
| O11 | **Photographability as a designed property** — test finishes under real aquascape LEDs, including Trophic AQ-LT lights | Needs a repeatable photo protocol | Design process |

### 4.1 Trade-off summary

| Tension | Aesthetic pull | Engineering pull | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Transparency vs cleanability | Glass disappears | Opaque steel is durable and restorable | Design + QA |
| Matte texture vs hygiene | Hides fingerprints and glints | Roughness may hold biofilm; blasting may lower passivation | QA, Bio, Mfg |
| Dark coating vs honest ageing | Recedes in scape | Chips; coating safety in water | QA, Bio, Mfg |
| Dark fasteners vs wet corrosion | Family rule | Black oxide rusts in water | Mfg, QA |
| Flared outlet vs flow target | Balanced proportion | Velocity, ripple, CO₂ loss | Sys, Bio |
| Serviceable joints vs crevices | Function as decoration | Biofilm, leaks, shrimp traps | QA, Bio, Sys |
| Legible intake guard vs calm surface | Honest slots | Geometry set by livestock safety and clogging | Bio, Sys |
| Laser mark vs corrosion | Small honest mark | Passive-layer damage | QA, Mfg |
| Distinct section vs brushability | Recognisable profile | Bending, standard brushes, losses | Mfg, Sys |
| Dark hose vs flow visibility | Ages better visually | Hides blockages; plasticisers | Sys, QA, Bio |

## 5. Design review of the proposed product options

The CMF specialist reviewed the launch options in [COMMERCIAL_FEASIBILITY.md](../../../products/aquarium/lily-pipes/costing/COMMERCIAL_FEASIBILITY.md) §2 on 2026-09-17. Summary of its recommendation (a recommendation, not a decision):

**P1 — serviceable stainless set: credible route to a Trophic language, with conditions.** It fills the gap where stainless has no language of its own, and nothing it adds is ornament.

- Strongest: a clip designed with the pipe (no competitor owns a clip form); one-piece bent tube with no wetted welds, so the only breaks in the form are the service joints; a defined rim transition; honest, restorable ageing as the customer promise.
- Weakest: finish still open, so the tonal family link is undefined — if testing ends with bright natural steel, P1 risks reading as "a generic steel set with a better clip"; no primary recognition cue yet; electropolish may drift toward mirror (glints, jewellery look); at ~1.8–2× a generic pair the difference must be visible **in a photo**, not only in the hand.
- **Conflict flagged (Mfg, Sys):** "no wetted welds" plus "termination derived from the flow job" means the outlet is either formed from the tube or a separate joined part. That choice determines the outlet's appearance and is not a CMF decision.

**Aesthetic risks and the evidence that retires them:**

| Risk | Retiring evidence |
|---|---|
| Industrial steel looks wrong in a nature scape | Photo protocol across three scape types and two backgrounds under AQ-LT lights; blind preference test vs generic stainless and glass; target C3 ≥ 4 in at least two of three scapes |
| Losing contest-photo buyers | Interviews sizing that group; accept the loss if small rather than compromising toward transparency |
| Finish ageing (chipping, tea staining, biofilm on texture) | Physical ageing board at 1/3/12 months of simulated maintenance in hard Coimbatore-type water and soft CO₂ water; new-vs-aged gap ≤ 3 weighted points; QA owns acceptance |
| Clip ages worse than the pipe (pad yellows, marks glass) | LED/UV exposure plus on/off cycle test of the contact material; glass-edge inspection |
| Green hose spoils the rim crossing | Month-3 photographs with clear vs tinted hose; decide whether Trophic specifies the hose |
| Two hose sizes do not share proportions | Side-by-side comparison before fixing the pilot size |
| Reserved skimmer interface looks like an unused stub | Close-up review of the intake with nothing attached |

**On the other options:**

- **P2 skimmer:** agrees it should be conditional; adds that it must also pass appearance gates (C3, C8, C12 at least equal to P1's intake). A float that works but looks cluttered fails.
- **P3 clip/guard accessory for existing pipes: partial disagreement.** Putting Trophic's signature clip on generic or glass pipes dilutes the cue before P1 establishes it. Sequence P3 after P1; a clip-only prototype may be used as research, not sold.
- **Glass rejected as first product:** agrees; keep "a non-metal where it earns its place" open for future accessories.
- **Private-label import rejected:** agrees; it cannot carry a design language.
- **One finish at launch**, not two; reconsider only if the photo test shows one finish failing a whole scape type.

Carried into [PRELIMINARY_REQUIREMENTS.md](../../../products/aquarium/lily-pipes/requirements/PRELIMINARY_REQUIREMENTS.md) as PROPOSED outcome requirements: O1, O3, O4, O5, O6 (rim crossing), O7, O10, O11. Kept open: O2 (finish family and relation to the lights), O8 (skimmer appearance, P2 only — except that P1's unused skimmer interface must look finished), O9 (section/grip cue), and whether Trophic supplies the hose.

## 6. Questions for the design phase

**Settle first (CMF priority order):**

1. **Where does recognisability live?** One primary cue — the clip, the rim collar, or the termination — with the others kept quiet.
2. **Which finish family can pass the in-water tests?** QA and Bio set roughness, passivation and coating-safety limits first; CMF chooses tone and gloss inside them, including how close electropolish may get to mirror.
3. **Who is the buyer at month 6?** Interviews separating owners who maintain their tanks from contest photographers set the C3/C12 vs C10 weighting.
4. **What does "good at month 12" look like, and how is it scored?** Agree the ageing board, photo protocol and QA thresholds before exploring forms.
5. **Is the hose part of the system?** The rim crossing cannot be resolved without deciding hose tone and whether Trophic specifies or supplies it.

**Also open:**

6. Does the in-water finish match the dry graphite lights, or relate by tone and detail while being a distinct "wet" finish?
7. Does the lights' dark-fastener rule apply to wet parts, or do wet parts adopt "no visible fasteners"?
8. Which shared details bind inflow, outflow, skimmer, clip and future wet accessories (drop checker, CO₂ diffuser) into one family without repeating a gimmick?
9. The anti-mimicry checklist: ADA P/V, ADA Poppy, Cal Aqua Labs funnel, Neo Flow modular joints, Chihiros slim stainless, Week Aqua curved steel.
10. Is a cleaning brush part of the product family?
11. What restoration routine can a user do at home without damaging the finish (and without bleach on stainless — see [LILY_PIPE_TECHNICAL_REFERENCE.md](LILY_PIPE_TECHNICAL_REFERENCE.md) §8.5)?

**Evidence gaps to close:** buy and physically score about seven reference sets (ADA P/V glass, ADA Metal Pipe, Chihiros stainless with skimmer, Neo Flow Premium, one Cal Aqua Labs piece, one generic stainless set with clear holders, one black-coated stainless set if one exists); confirm Twinstar and "VCS"; review real hobbyist photographs for glints, dark pipes in bright scapes and hose ageing. Pinterest for discovery only, never to copy.

## Appendix A — Scoring anchors (1–5) for C1–C12

For every criterion, **3 = competent category norm** (a decent generic set). The anchors
say what moves a score up or down.

**C1 Proportion**
1. A generic tube cut to length. The outlet or intake is visibly too big or too small for the tube. The part looks stretched or stubby at the stated tank size.
2. Acceptable at one tank size only. The inflow and outflow were clearly sized separately.
3. Balanced, conventional category proportions.
4. Deliberate ratios that hold across a size range. The inflow and outflow feel sized for each other.
5. Proportions that read as engineered for the scape scale, with a family rhythm between sizes and between inflow, outflow and skimmer. Nothing looks arbitrary.

**C2 Silhouette and section**
1. Muddled outline with add-on parts breaking it, or a near-copy of a signature competitor form.
2. Generic "lily" outline, indistinguishable from dozens of copies.
3. A clean conventional outline.
4. A clear, calm outline with one distinctive, functional cue.
5. Recognisable at a glance as its own family through section or termination detail, while staying calm. It cannot be mistaken for ADA, Chihiros, Neo and others.

**C3 Visual weight in water**
1. The eye goes to the pipe before the scape. Strong glints, high contrast, bulky parts.
2. Noticeable in most scapes. Distracting under strong lights.
3. Visible but tolerable. It fades in dense planting.
4. Recedes in most scape types. Glints are controlled.
5. Present only when looked for, from both front and top, under full light. "Quiet" does not depend on perfect cleanliness.

**C4 Material and finish**
1. A coating or imitation finish (plastic pretending to be metal or glass), patchy gloss, visible tooling marks.
2. Honest material, but the finish is inconsistent from part to part or batch to batch.
3. Uniform, appropriate finish.
4. The finish is chosen for the job (glare, grip, cleanability) and reads as considered close up.
5. Material and finish tell the construction story and relate clearly to the Trophic family (graphite lights) without matching for its own sake. Seen close, it rewards inspection.

**C5 Bends and transitions**
1. Wrinkled or ovalised bends, blobby welds, visible glue, mismatched diameters.
2. Clean bends, but joints are visible and untreated.
3. Clean bends. Joints are neat but unconsidered.
4. Consistent radii. Joints are placed where they read as intended.
5. Every transition (bend, swage, joint, termination) is resolved and matches the others. A seam, where there is one, is expressed and aligned, not hidden badly.

**C6 Mounting hardware**
1. Clear suction cups that yellow, oversized clips, bright zinc screws, parts that visibly bodge onto rimless glass.
2. Works, but reads as a separate cheaper product.
3. A neat single clip that is visually neutral.
4. Few parts, dark or neutral, sized to the glass. Mounting and pipe look designed together.
5. "Mounting elegance": minimal contact, obvious how it works, adjustable without tools or with one honest fastener. Dark, few fasteners. Reads as a detail worth photographing.

**C7 Hose interface**
1. Green or grey stock hose pushed over a barb, a visible hose clamp, kinks at the rim.
2. Clear hose that goes green quickly, with a lumpy barb bulge.
3. Clean push-fit with a neutral hose.
4. A defined transition (sleeve, collar or step). Hose colour chosen with the pipe.
5. A resolved rim crossing: the eye reads pipe, collar, hose as one line. Hose colour and ageing are considered, and the system still looks good when the hose clouds.

**C8 Inflow/outflow consistency**
1. Unrelated parts, often from different sets or materials.
2. Same material, different design language.
3. A matched pair.
4. A matched pair plus skimmer, with shared mount and termination language.
5. A family that extends clearly to accessories (skimmer, cleaning brush, holders, drop checker and so on) without repeating one gimmick.

**C9 Scape complementarity**
1. Clashes in most scapes (for example bright steel in a dark jungle, green plastic anywhere).
2. Suits one scape type only.
3. Neutral in most scapes.
4. Actively suits several scape types and backgrounds.
5. Looks at home beside wood, stone, plants and glass in bright and dark scapes, and the finish choice adds to the "maker" character of the setup.

**C10 Appearance under real use**
1. Degrades visibly within weeks, degradation reads as neglect or failure (peeling coating, rust bloom, cracks, yellow cups), and it cannot be restored.
2. Degrades quickly but can be restored with effort or a special brush.
3. Normal category ageing, restorable with normal tools.
4. Degrades slowly, and the first signs are subtle. Easy full restore.
5. Wear looks intentional (Leatherman/Knipex principle). Deposits show little or clean off with simple tools. After a year it looks used-and-cared-for, not tired.

**C11 Maintenance tactility**
1. Fragile anxiety, sharp edges, unclear how to take it apart, cannot be brushed.
2. Can be cleaned but awkward. Small parts get lost.
3. Can be cleaned with a standard lily-pipe brush.
4. Comes apart obviously. Grip points are clear. Parts cannot be refitted wrongly.
5. A pleasure to service: confident weight, clear disassembly cues, every internal surface reachable, parts that feel like tools.

**C12 Photographability**
1. Must be removed for any serious photo (hot glints, hose colour, suction cups).
2. Can be photographed only if freshly cleaned and carefully angled.
3. Acceptable in full-tank shots.
4. Photographs well in full-tank shots, and holds up in macro shots.
5. Enhances close-up and product photography. No distracting reflections in the front glass. Hobbyists would happily leave it in a contest photo.
