## Part A — Fundamentals (durable)

### A1. Radiometric, photometric and photon quantities

| Quantity | Symbol / unit | Meaning | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Radiant flux | W | Total optical power emitted | Efficiency, thermal budgets |
| Luminous flux | lm | Radiant flux weighted by the human eye's V(λ) curve | Aquarium *visual* brightness; meaningless for plants |
| Photosynthetic photon flux, PPF | µmol/s | Photons emitted between 400–700 nm per second | The fixture's plant-relevant output |
| Photosynthetic photon flux density, PPFD | µmol/m²/s | PPF arriving per m² of surface | Plant-relevant *intensity* at the canopy or substrate |
| Daily light integral, DLI | mol/m²/day | PPFD integrated over the photoperiod: DLI = PPFD × hours × 3600 / 10⁶ | The plant's daily "dose"; the number that actually drives growth |
| Photon efficacy | µmol/J | PPF per watt of *electrical input* | The efficiency figure for horticulture. Fixture-level includes driver loss; LED-level does not |
| Luminous efficacy | lm/W | Lumens per electrical watt | Visual efficiency; do not compare with µmol/J |
| Spectral power distribution, SPD | W/nm or relative | Power vs wavelength | Everything about colour and spectrum starts here |
| PAR | 400–700 nm band | Photosynthetically active radiation | The band PPF/PPFD count. Far-red (700–750 nm) is outside PAR but photobiologically active; "ePAR" (400–750) is sometimes used |

Worked relationships (fact):

- A 660 nm photon carries 3.01 × 10⁻¹⁹ J, so 1 W at 660 nm ≈ 5.52 µmol/s; at 450 nm ≈ 3.76 µmol/s. **Red photons are cheaper per watt than blue** by ~47 %, which is why red-heavy horticultural spectra score high µmol/J.
- White LEDs of ~4000 K, 80 CRI convert at roughly 4.5–4.8 µmol/s per optical watt; the photon efficacy of a white *fixture* is therefore typically 2.3–3.0 µmol/J for mid-power 2024–2026 packages including driver loss (datasheet-derived, inference).
- Lux → PPFD conversion depends entirely on SPD. For white LEDs ~ 0.015–0.02 µmol/m²/s per lux; for red/blue mixes the factor is far higher. Never use a lux meter as a PAR meter without a calibrated factor for the exact spectrum.
- Inverse-square applies to a point source only; a linear bar at close range behaves more like a line source (falls off ~1/d) until distance exceeds the bar length. Close-canopy uniformity is set by geometry and optics, not by total flux.

### A2. LED engineering

**Packages.** Mid-power SMD (2835, 3030, 3535, 5050) dominate linear luminaires: low cost, many emitters, wide beam (~120°), low thermal density per package. High-power (3535 ceramic, 5050) and COB give higher flux per point at higher thermal density and cost. Colour and horticultural emitters (660 nm deep red, 450 nm royal blue, 730 nm far-red) come mainly in 3030/3535 ceramic packages. RGB/RGBW multi-die packages put 3–4 dies under one lens for mixing at short distance.

**Binning.** Manufacturers sort emitters by flux, forward voltage (Vf), and chromaticity (for whites, ANSI/MacAdam ellipses; 3-step SDCM is visually uniform, 5–7-step is not for adjacent emitters on one bar). A product must specify the accepted bins, and a distributor must ship them; unbinned or "mixed bin" reels are the normal counterfeit/grey-market symptom. Colour LEDs bin by dominant wavelength (±5 nm typical steps); this matters for RGB mixing consistency between units.

**Forward voltage and current.** Typical Vf at nominal current: phosphor white 2.7–3.2 V; blue 2.9–3.3 V; green (InGaN) 2.9–3.4 V; red (AlInGaP) 1.9–2.3 V; far-red 1.7–2.1 V. Vf falls ~2 mV/K as junction temperature rises, and varies between bins by ±0.1–0.2 V. Light output is roughly linear with current below nominal and sub-linear above it ("droop"), so **efficacy is highest at low current density** — running many emitters at a fraction of rated current is the standard way to buy efficacy and lifetime with LED count.

**Series/parallel topology.** A constant-current (CC) driver sets one current through a series string; the string voltage is ΣVf. Parallel strings share current only if their ΣVf match, so parallel strings need either matched bins, ballast resistors, or per-string regulators. **Mixed emitter types on one string** (white + red in series) work — the current is common and the string voltage is the sum — but the ratio of white to red is then fixed by emitter count, not adjustable. Mixed types in parallel strings do **not** share current predictably.

**Thermal.** Junction temperature Tj = T_ambient + P_heat × (Rth_j-sp + Rth_sp-board + Rth_board-heatsink + Rth_heatsink-ambient). Datasheet lifetime (L70, L80, L90) is stated at a specific Tj and current per IES LM-80 test data, extrapolated by TM-21; the extrapolation is limited to 6× test duration. Every 10 K rise in Tj roughly halves the time to a given lumen depreciation, and raises Vf drift, phosphor degradation and colour shift. Fixture thermal design is therefore about a low Rth chain and a realistic ambient (an Indian summer room at 35–40 °C, or a 4-tier rack where heat rises).

**Degradation and failure modes.** Gradual: lumen depreciation, phosphor browning, colour shift (whites drift towards blue as phosphor degrades or towards yellow as the encapsulant yellows), Vf drift. Abrupt: bond-wire fatigue from thermal cycling, ESD damage to blue/green InGaN dies (classic assembly failure), solder-joint cracking on MCPCB from CTE mismatch and thermal cycling, moisture-induced corrosion of silver plating in packages exposed to sulfur or VOCs (aquarium water treatments, adhesives), and delamination. A single open emitter in a series string takes the whole string out unless the driver or a bypass element handles it.

**MCPCB.** Aluminium-core PCB: copper foil → thermally conductive dielectric (1–3 W/m·K standard, up to ~8 for premium) → aluminium base (1.0–1.6 mm typical). Single-layer copper, so no vias and limited routing: LED strings run in simple series chains and control electronics go on a separate FR-4 board. The dielectric layer dominates thermal resistance; thin high-k dielectric costs more. Standard panel sizes limit single-piece board length (commonly ≤ 1.2 m from specialist shops, ≤ ~500–600 mm from generalists), so long bars are tiled from modules. White solder mask raises optical efficiency a few percent. Surface finishes: ENIG for fine pitch and shelf life, OSP cheaper but with a short assembly window. Reflow of LEDs on MCPCB needs the board's thermal mass in the profile and attention to MSL (moisture sensitivity) of the LED packages.

### A3. Control

**Constant-current driving.** LEDs are current devices; the driver regulates current, and the string voltage follows. A remote CC driver delivers a fixed current over the cable to a string whose voltage may be tens of volts (safe ELV if kept below 60 V DC). A constant-voltage (CV) bus instead delivers a fixed voltage, and each fixture carries its own CC stage (buck if the bus exceeds the string voltage, boost if below, buck-boost if either). The choice between "CC over the cable" and "CV bus + local CC" decides where the driver sits, what the connector carries, and whether the fixture is dumb or smart.

**Dimming.** *PWM* switches the LED at full current with a variable duty cycle: colour point stays constant, efficacy stays constant, but the pulse frequency must be high enough to avoid visible flicker, camera banding and stroboscopic effect (IEEE 1789 gives guidance; ≥ 1–3 kHz is typical for camera-friendly aquarium lights, and bit depth of ≥ 10–12 bits is needed for smooth low-end fades). *Analogue* (constant-current reduction) dims by lowering the DC current: no flicker, higher efficacy at low current, but the colour point of phosphor whites shifts and colour LEDs shift wavelength at low current, and deep dimming below ~5–10 % is hard. *Hybrid* schemes use analogue in the upper range and PWM below. **A mixed-LED single channel changes colour when analogue-dimmed and does not when PWM-dimmed** — an important reason a fixed-spectrum mixed engine tends to use PWM.

**0–10 V (and 1–10 V).** The building-lighting dimming interface: the driver sources a small current (typically ≤ 2 mA) on a control pair and the controller sinks it to a voltage; 10 V is full, and the low end is either off (0–10 V, IEC 60929 Annex E.2 style) or minimum-not-off (1–10 V). Curves are linear or logarithmic per driver model. Behaviour when the control pair is open (usually full output) or shorted (usually minimum or off) is driver-specific and matters for fail-safe. **Whether "dim to zero" switches the LED off or leaves it at minimum current is a driver-model property**, which is why the rack's open "driver model" decision governs how photoperiod-off is implemented.

**Multichannel control.** Each independently controllable spectrum channel needs its own CC regulator, its own PWM/analogue signal, and either its own control pair (analogue interfaces) or a digital bus (a microcontroller with n PWM outputs, DALI DT8 in building lighting, or a proprietary BLE/Wi-Fi app protocol in aquarium products). Channel count therefore multiplies driver stages, board area, connector pins (in remote-driver architectures), firmware complexity and validation work; it is never free.

**Scheduling and power-failure recovery.** A standalone light needs a real-time clock and a persistent schedule. After a power cut the clock must still be right (backup battery or supercapacitor, or a re-sync from a phone or network) or the light will run its programme at the wrong time. The fail-safe state on loss of control signal is a design choice: for a CEA room "full on" wastes energy and can overheat; "off" loses a crop; the choice must be explicit. For an aquarium the safe state on loss of control is "resume last schedule" not "full on" (algae, fish stress).

### A4. Optics

- **Bare mid-power boards** give a Lambertian ~120° distribution; uniformity at close range depends on emitter pitch relative to mounting height (pitch ≲ height gives smooth output). Edge fall-off is intrinsic; fixtures are usually made longer than the target area or optics are added.
- **Lenses** (TIR, per-emitter or lens arrays) narrow the beam, raise on-axis PPFD and reduce spill, at the cost of ~8–15 % transmission loss, hot-spotting if misapplied, and tooling. Batwing lens profiles specifically flatten close-range uniformity.
- **Diffusers / covers** (PMMA, PC, frosted or micro-prismatic) blend adjacent emitters, hide the LED grid, spread the source and lose 8–20 % depending on haze. PC yellows under UV and blue-rich light and heat faster than PMMA; PMMA is more brittle and scratches. Glass is stable but heavy and costly.
- **Reflectors** recover side-emitted light; effective on high-power sources, marginal on wide-beam mid-power boards.
- **Uniformity** is stated as CV (σ/mean) or min/avg over a measurement grid; the grid spacing and the area over which it is calculated must be stated or the figure is meaningless.
- **Glare and shimmer (aquarium).** Point sources (few high-power emitters, bare lenses) produce the "shimmer" ripple on the substrate that many aquascapers like, and produce glare and a visible LED grid; diffused sources give even, calm light with no shimmer. These are opposing preferences and a product picks a point on that line.
- **Colour mixing (RGB/WRGB).** Discrete R, G, B emitters produce coloured shadows and fringes unless mixed: close pitch, a mixing distance before the cover, a diffuser, or multi-die packages. Mixing quality is judged on a white surface below the fixture at the working distance.
- **Mounting height** sets both intensity and uniformity; a fixture is designed for a working-distance band (CEA: 150 mm here; aquarium: typically 15–30 cm above the water surface plus water depth to the substrate) and must be validated at that band.

### A5. CEA-specific concerns

PPFD and DLI set yield; uniformity sets crop repeatability tray to tray; efficacy sets electricity and HVAC cost (every watt not converted to photons is heat the room must remove); photoperiod interacts with both. Morphology (compactness, hypocotyl length, leaf area) responds to blue fraction and to far-red. Operational reliability matters more than in a living room: the fixture runs 16–24 h/day in a humid room above flood trays, at canopy level, and a failure costs a crop cycle. Serviceability is a facility requirement: a bar must be swappable without electrical work at mains voltage.

### A6. Aquarium-specific concerns

Two goals coexist: the plant's photosynthesis (PAR at the substrate, after water attenuation) and the human's view (colour rendering of plants, fish and hardscape, saturation of reds and greens, no ugly tint, no visible LED grid, shimmer preference). High CRI whites render faithfully but "flat"; RGB-enriched spectra over-saturate reds and greens, which the market reads as vivid. The water surface adds reflection loss at the air–water interface (~2–4 % at normal incidence, more at grazing angles), refraction, and ripple-driven shimmer. Red is attenuated more than blue per metre of water but at 30–45 cm depth in clear water the effect is small compared with tannin absorption (which removes blue first). Glare to a viewer seated beside the tank is a design constraint on fixture height and cover choice. Condensation on the underside, splash from water changes and salt/mineral creep on connectors are the dominant environmental stresses.

### A7. Environmental and reliability

| Stress | Mechanism | Design and test response |
|---|---|---|
| Humidity, condensation | Moisture on PCB and connector; corrosion, leakage current, dendrite growth | Conformal coating, sealed or drained enclosure design, IP tests, damp-heat cycling |
| Splash and water exposure | Direct water on cover, end caps, connector | IPX4-class splash tests; drip edges; drained not "sealed" designs that trap water |
| Thermal derating | Tj rise in hot ambient or stacked racks | Thermal test at 35–40 °C ambient; current derating with temperature in firmware or driver |
| Corrosion | Aquarium fertiliser splash, salt creep, sulfur-bearing water treatments attacking silver plating and anodise | Material choice, plating spec, salt-fog test for coastal customers |
| Seal ageing | Gasket compression set, UV/heat embrittlement of polymer | Material spec (silicone > EPDM > TPE for heat), compression-set test |
| Electrical faults | Reverse polarity, wrong adapter, surge from pumps/heaters on the same strip, ESD during handling | Input protection design; IEC 61000-4-2/-4-5 pre-tests |
| LED and driver failure | Open string, driver IC overheat, capacitor ageing | Derated components, string-fault detection, temperature monitoring |
| Cover ageing | PC/PMMA yellowing under blue and heat | Material choice; accelerated UV/heat exposure |

Standards commonly relevant (fact, applicability to a given product is a later determination): IEC 62368-1 / IS 13252 Part 1 (ICT/AV equipment safety, which BIS applies to power adapters), IEC 61347 / IS 15885 (LED control gear), IEC 60598 / IS 10322 (luminaires), IEC 62471 (photobiological safety), IEC 60529 (IP ratings), IEC 60068-2 (environmental tests), CISPR 15 / IEC 61000 (EMC of lighting), IES LM-79 (fixture photometry), LM-80/TM-21 (LED lumen maintenance), IEEE 1789 (flicker).

**Remote driver at 48 V, the three readings.** RK-A-SYS §07 states "Supply to
fixture: constant-voltage 48 V DC, or constant-current with output ≤ 60 V" [fact,
verified in the rack spec 2026-09-11]. That permits both of the following and
the choice is the rack's open "driver model" decision:

- **(a) Remote CC driver per bar, string ≤ 48 V class.** Bar is passive (LEDs and board only). Two bars per tier means two drivers on one 0–10 V pair, which is fine (each driver sources ~100 µA on the dim pair [datasheet]). Hidden hazard: a CC driver's **open-circuit voltage appears at the IP65 connector when a bar is unplugged**. Mean Well XLG-150-L OCV is 225 V, -M 115 V, -H 60 V [datasheet]. Only H-class (≤ 60 V OCV) drivers keep the canopy connector inside ELV; L/M-class violate the ELV boundary even though the running string is under 48 V. This must be a driver selection criterion [inference].
- **(b) 48 V CV bus + CC regulator on the bar.** Contradicts "driver not at the fixture"; a buck CC stage on the board is control gear under IEC 61347-2-13 in substance and adds a switching EMI source at canopy. Only reachable through an ICR on the rack interface.
- **(c) One remote CC driver per tier feeding two bars.** Series gives ~88 V (outside class); parallel needs matched Vf and per-bar current sharing, and one bar failing open doubles current in the other [inference]. Feasible with care; (a) is safer.

String arithmetic [illustrative]: 14–16 whites at 2.75 V fit under 48 V with Vf drift; 20–22 reds at 2.0–2.2 V. A bar of 11 parallel strings × 16 whites at 65 mA is ~44 V / 0.7 A; 22 strings ~1.4 A, inside XLG-150-H (27–56 V, 1.4–4.17 A) or HLG-150H-48B envelopes [datasheet]. Cable loss on 3 m of 1.0 mm² at 1.4 A ≈ 0.16 V, negligible. An M12 A-coded 4-pin connector (typ 4 A) suffices for one CC pair; a CV bus would want L-coded 16 A [inference, NEEDS VALIDATION].

**Indicative power class** [illustrative, not spec]. Bed 1.176 × 0.569 = 0.669 m². Canopy flux at 150/180/210 µmol/m²/s = 100/120/140 µmol/s. With an optical capture fraction of 0.65–0.85 [assumption], fixture PPF ≈ 120–215 µmol/s per tier, 60–110 per bar. At 2.3–2.7 µmol/J realistic: **~25–50 W per bar, 50–100 W per tier, 200–400 W LED load per rack** plus 7–9 % remote-driver loss. Heat per bar 15–30 W; a finned 40 × 60 × 1100 mm profile of 0.35–0.5 m² at 8–12 W/m²K gives ΔT 5–10 K sink-to-air [inference], so passive cooling is plausible. Tier air: 100 W into a 178 m³/h fan flow (≈ 60 W/K) is ~1.5–2 K, so the ≤ 3 K acceptance is **tight at reduced fan speed with four stacked tiers — NEEDS VALIDATION**.

**Optics and the CV ≤ 15 % target.** Bar centres at Y 178 and 382 mm, height 150 mm, bed 0–569 mm. For bare Lambertian line sources (irradiance ∝ cos³θ): under a bar ≈ 1.21 relative, midpoint ≈ 1.13, bed front/rear edges ≈ 0.31 [inference]. Edges at 25–30 % of centre put CV far above 15 % before long-axis end fall-off. The EDR-003 row positions are ~180 mm inboard of each edge, so the bars must throw light outward. Options: asymmetric/batwing linear lenses (Ledil, Khatod, extruded PMMA/PC; local optical-grade extrusion NEEDS VALIDATION); bent-aluminium specular reflectors (Alanod-type ~95 %), locally fabricable and the best fit to a 40 mm envelope [inference]; opal diffuser (costs 10–15 % flux, does not fix edges); white tray sidewalls or edge baffles (cheapest lever, needs rack-side agreement). Realistic fixture-only outcome with lenses or reflectors: CV 10–20 %; ≤ 15 % likely needs edge reflectors [inference]. Validation: quantum sensor on a jig, ≥ 7 × 4 grid at the 139 mm bed datum plus a 50 mm border, and a ray-trace against measured LED intensity before tooling.

**MCPCB.** 1100 mm single boards exist but yield, warp and CTE mismatch argue for 2–3 segments of ~360–560 mm bridged by board-to-board connectors [inference]; Indian fab panel limits RESEARCH REQUIRED. FR-4 2 oz with thermal via arrays is often adequate for 3030 whites at ≤ 100 mA and cheaper than MCPCB; Oslon-class reds at 350–700 mA push toward MCPCB. LED-grade white mask yellows under heat; specify grade and verify after ageing. ENIG if re-population is intended. **Re-populatable fixed spectrum**: reserve every k-th position with a dual footprint (3030 white / 3030 red, or 3030/3535) in the same series string, with driver CC-window headroom so swapping 2.0 V reds for 2.8 V whites stays in range. High-current reds go on a separate fixed-ratio string with its own driver, still not tunable.

**Control facts.** IEC 60929 Annex E is a current-sink interface (driver sources 10 µA–2 mA; 1 V is minimum); ESTA E1.3 is current-source and incompatible [fact]. Mean Well HLG-B is 1–10 V, 10 % minimum, no dim-to-off; XLG-AB is 0–10 V dim-to-off with output undefined below 8 % [datasheet]. With a sourcing driver, **open dim wires → 10 V → full output; shorted → off (XLG) or 10 % (HLG)**. Full-on on lost signal is crop-safe only if the driver's current setting caps 100 % at ≤ 210 µmol/m²/s at canopy, so **hardware, not software, enforces the PPFD ceiling — NEEDS DECISION**. HLG-class needs a relay for photoperiod-off; XLG-class can dim to off but its dead band and standby power should be checked against the software's `light.dim` semantics. Candidate driver families with 48 V-class CC region, 0–10 V, ≤ 60 V OCV: Mean Well XLG-75/100/150-H-AB (IP67, IS 15885 mark, Indian distribution), HLG-xxH-48B/54B (1–10 V), ELG-xx-48B; Inventronics EUM/EUG; Tridonic/Osram (largely DALI). Indian OEM drivers are mostly indoor non-dimmable, RESEARCH REQUIRED.

**Standards (research findings, not interpretation).** IS 15885 Pt 2/Sec 13 = IEC 61347-2-13 applies to the remote mains driver and sits under BIS CRS [fact, multiple certifier sources]; IS 10322 Pt 5/Sec 1 = IEC 60598-2-1 for luminaires is under CRS, and whether a DC-only ELV bar with no control gear is a CRS "luminaire" or an LED module (IS 16104 = IEC 62031; IS 16103 = IEC 62717) is RESEARCH REQUIRED with BIS counsel; IS 16108 / IEC 62471 photobiological classification is needed for a blue-rich 660 nm fixture viewed at 150–300 mm [inference]; EMC (IS 6842 / CISPR 15, IEC 61547, IEC 61000-3-2 Class C) sits with the mains driver, and a passive DC bar has no emissions of its own.

**Tunability cost, as a conflict analysis.** N channels means N CC outputs per bar, N remote drivers per bar (8N per rack) or multichannel drivers, N 0–10 V pairs per tier against the frozen one, a 1+N or 2N pin connector at lower current rating, N string groups on the MCPCB, and an N-dimensional `light.dim` in `trophic-contracts`. Every one crosses a frozen rack interface, so tunability is ICR territory. Non-colliding options: fixed-ratio dual string (white + red on two drivers sharing the tier pair), or a Gen-2 digital dimming interface via its own ICR.

### B2. Aquarium Core fixed-spectrum engine classes (`AQ-LT-A` context)

Researched 2026-09-11 by the Lighting/Electronics specialist. Sources at the end of B4.

**Physics that governs every choice [peer-reviewed, Kusuma, Pattison & Bugbee 2020].** Package-level efficiency at 25 °C: blue 450 nm 93 % wall-plug / 3.5 µmol/J; red 660 nm 81 % / 4.5 µmol/J; phosphor white 6500 K 76 % / 2.9 µmol/J, 2700 K 69 % / 2.6; direct green 530 nm 42 % / 1.9 (the "green gap"). Real fixtures land at 2.5–3.0 µmol/J. Photopic V(λ) at 660 nm ≈ 0.06 versus ≈ 0.32 at 625 nm [fact, CIE 1931], so 660 nm looks 5× dimmer per watt: it barely moves the eye's impression but dominates red PAR and, mixed with white, pushes red pigment saturation at little lumen cost. Deep-red AlInGaP flux falls to ~50 % at Tj 100 °C while InGaN royal blue holds ~88 % [datasheet, Cree XE-G], so any white+red mix drifts cooler as the board heats unless the thermal design holds Tj down.

**Mixed LED types on one channel, the trade-off [inference].** *Series mixing* (all types in one string, one CC driver): identical current through every die, ratio set by die count not Vf. Colour still shifts with dimming: PWM keeps peak current constant so the ratio holds from 1–100 % (preferred); analogue dimming drops red and green external efficiency differently from blue and white and shifts CCT. Series mixing also shifts with temperature (red −50 % at Tj 100 °C); accept or manage thermally. *Parallel strings of unlike Vf* on one driver divide current by I–V curve, risk thermal runaway of the lower-Vf red string, and wander with temperature; avoid unless each string has its own regulator, which is a multichannel driver by another name. A single-channel design avoids the problem with one string (or several identical strings each on its own CC channel), PWM ≥ 3 kHz, and a fixed die mix. String count is a bus-voltage question: at 24 V a string is ~7 whites or ~9 reds; at 48 V ~14 whites (see Part C).

**Evaluation criteria for Core.** TM-30-20 Rf and Rg plus the red and green hue-bin chroma shifts Rcs,h1 and Rcs,h9 (saturation direction); CRI Ra and R9; CCT and Duv (Duv below −0.003 reads magenta over a tank [field]); PPFD at water surface and substrate for 30 and 45 cm; PPFD per watt; spectral stability from 10–100 % dimming; CCT drift from 25 to 60 °C board; BOM cost per class; distributor lead time; second-source availability.

**Counterfeit and binning [field].** Nichia, Samsung, Osram, Lumileds and Bridgelux are all counterfeited on grey channels; buy only through franchised distribution (Mouser India, Arrow, Avnet, WPG) or direct with certificate of conformance. Chinese Sanan and Hongli are legitimate at direct-from-factory MOQ (reels of 4,000–10,000; one reel per type covers 200 units). Binning: 3-step MacAdam or tighter for white is a Trophic assumption that NEEDS DECISION; 660 nm peak-wavelength bin ±5 nm minimum.

### B3. WRGB engine architectures (`AQ-LT-B` context)

**Package options [datasheet, inference].**

- *Discrete high-power colour on a common footprint* (Cree XLamp XE-G 2.05 × 1.6 mm, Luxeon Rubix 1.4 × 1.4 mm, both to 3 A). XE-G at 1 A, 25 °C: royal blue 1500–1600 mW at 3.0 V; green 340–400 lm at 2.75 V, 520–535 nm; red 156–172 lm at 2.4 V, 620–630 nm; photo red 1100–1200 mW at 2.25 V, 650–670 nm; white 340–400 lm at 2.9 V; Rth 1.9–2.6 K/W. One footprint for every colour → one MCPCB layout, reusable optics; highest cost per die, lowest count per fixture.
- *Multi-die RGB(W) packages* (Osram Multi-LED, Chinese 3535/5050 RGBW): compact mixing at the source, lower per-point cost, each die still needs its own channel, coarser die-level binning; Chinese 5050 RGBW long-term bin continuity is the sourcing risk [field].
- *Mid-power discrete R/G/B/W 2835/3030*: cheapest per lumen, dense arrays, better mixing by count, more solder joints; matches the Chihiros "4-in-1" architecture (marketed as white + R + G + B in one chip, BLE app) [mfr claim].

**Channel count [inference].** 3-channel RGB cannot make an efficient, high-CRI white: white-from-RGB is limited by green efficiency and gives Rf < 80. 4-channel WRGB, where white carries the mid-day lumen and PAR load and RGB adds saturation and hue, is the industry norm and the efficiency-sane choice. A 5th channel (660 nm deep red, or royal blue, or UVA) buys pigment appearance or shimmer but adds a driver, a bin, a PWM output and mixing complexity. Planning the driver stage as **4 mandatory + 1 optional** channel lets the decision follow bench SPD tests; NEEDS DECISION.

**660 vs 620–630 nm red.** 630 nm gives ~5× the lumens per watt for visual red; 660 gives more red PAR and deeper pigment saturation but needs 2–3× the radiant power for the same visual effect [fact + inference]. A common practice is 620–630 nm for the R channel plus a 660 nm share on the white or 5th channel; test both.

**Green.** 520–535 nm InGaN, Vf bin spread 2.6–3.4 V [datasheet], lowest wall-plug efficiency (~40 %), heaviest droop; needs its own CC channel (never shared with red); green bin spread is what makes unit-to-unit hue mismatch visible at 10–20 units, so specify a single flux and wavelength bin per build lot.

**Colour mixing.** At 15–30 cm mounting heights, discrete dies on ≥ 10 mm pitch produce coloured shadows and edges on hardscape [field]. Options: dense small-pitch RGBW array under a light diffuser (cheap, 10–20 % loss); per-die lens array with a common beam (higher cost, keeps sparkle); a shallow mixing chamber. Measure colour uniformity (Δu'v' across the substrate) in the pilot; NEEDS VALIDATION. Diffuser vs bare-lens shimmer is an aesthetic decision that also drives thermal and optical loss (flag to Industrial Design).

**Drivers and dimming.** One CC channel per colour; ≥ 12-bit PWM for smooth sunrise at low levels (8-bit steps are visible below 5 %); PWM ≥ 3 kHz to sit in the IEEE 1789 "no effect" region and avoid banding on phone video [standard summary + inference]. 12-bit at 3.9 kHz is what a 16 MHz PWM clock yields (nRF52840: 16 MHz base, 4 × 4 channels [datasheet]); ESP32-C3 LEDC gives 6 channels to 14-bit, ~4.9 kHz at 14-bit from 80 MHz [datasheet + inference]. Hybrid PWM plus analogue trim keeps colour ratio stable while extending the low end. Current precision ±3 % per channel is adequate; ratio precision matters more than absolute.

**Thermal.** High-tech output (order 100–200 µmol/m²/s at substrate in a 60 cm tank [field, illustrative only]) with RGB at ~40–60 % wall-plug means roughly two-thirds of electrical input becomes heat in a slim bar: expect 15–40 W class dissipation over 30–60 cm and an extruded heatsink body holding Tj ≤ 85 °C to keep red and green drift tolerable [inference]. RGB needs roughly 30–50 % more input power than phosphor white for the same lumens when green and red do the work.

**Sourcing at 10–20 units.** Cree, Lumileds and Osram colour parts via Mouser or Arrow India in cut-tape quantities is realistic; consistent bins across a pilot require one reel per colour. Chinese RGBW direct needs MOQ negotiation and gives weak bin documentation. The colour BOM per fixture is several times the Core white BOM [inference].

**UVA, engineering assessment.** 365–400 nm emitters are cheap (Chinese 3535) but: lower wall-plug efficiency than blue, phosphor-free so no rendering benefit; polycarbonate, acrylic, silicone and PMMA yellow and embrittle under UVA (materials NEED VALIDATION if ever used); IEC 62471 adds UV-A eye and actinic hazard evaluations and IEC 62471-2 requires a "NOTICE UV emitted" label from RG1 upward [standard summary]; a dedicated driver channel is needed. Combined with the absence of a biological case (Part D2): **exclude UVA from both products unless evidence appears**; if a violet feature is ever wanted, 400–405 nm is visible and avoids UV labelling. NEEDS DECISION only if reopened.

**Blue-light hazard, both products.** Small-source blue LEDs at 470 nm and only 5–20 cd can already reach RG2, and white-LED hazard weighting rises with CCT [datasheet, Osram AN002]; a 6500 K + royal blue bar viewed at 200 mm needs an IEC 62471 assessment, NEEDS VALIDATION.

### B4. Shared control electronics (Core and WRGB)

**Common by nature [inference]:** MCU and radio module, RTC, DC input protection (reverse polarity, TVS, fuse, inrush), the DC connector (conditional on a shared bus voltage, Part C), buttons and status LED, dimming and schedule firmware with channel count as a configuration constant, the BLE app protocol, PCB stack-up rules, conformal-coating spec. **Inherently different:** driver channel count (1 vs 4–5), driver current class, MCPCB layout and LED bins, heatsink mass, PSU rating, IEC 62471 outcome.

**MCU classes [datasheet].** ESP32-C3: Wi-Fi b/g/n + BLE 5, 6-channel 14-bit LEDC, 5 µA deep sleep, external 32 kHz crystal, 4/8 MB flash, −40 to 85/105 °C; cheapest path to a phone app; WROOM modules carry regional radio approvals; single-source (Espressif) but high volume. Nordic nRF52832/840: BLE only, best sleep current, 16 PWM channels, costlier, never Wi-Fi. STM32/GD32/WCH plus a separate BLE module: most timer control and second-sourcing (GD32 pin-compatible), but two chips and two firmwares. BLE-only vs Wi-Fi: a standalone light with a local schedule needs only BLE; Wi-Fi adds NTP re-sync, cloud/voice, OTA convenience and a larger support surface. ESP32-C3 leaves Wi-Fi as a firmware-enabled option without a hardware change, a reasonable Phase 0 hedge; NEEDS DECISION.

**Schedule persistence and power-failure recovery [inference].** The schedule in MCU flash is trivial; *time* is the problem. Indian grid outages make (a) the natural default for Core [field + assumption].

**Indian certification for a radio product (consultant-source claims; verify with a BIS/WPC consultant, RESEARCH REQUIRED).** WPC ETA is required for 2.4 GHz BLE/Wi-Fi; a self-declaration route via Saral Sanchar applies from 9 Sep 2024 for delicensed bands; whether an Indian-built product using an already-ETA'd module needs its own ETA is stated inconsistently across consultants. ## Part C — Power and control platform research (2026-09-11)

Researched 2026-09-11 by the Lighting/Electronics specialist. Applies to the aquarium platform (`AQ-LT-A`, `AQ-LT-B`); `LT-A` is fixed at the rack's 48 V class by RK-A-SYS §07. Sources at the end of Part C. **No bus voltage is chosen here.**

### C1. Derivation method and the 24 / 36 / 48 V comparison

**Method (LED → driver → system → supply).** (a) Fix Vf classes and dies per channel per size class. (b) Choose driver topology from the string-voltage to bus-voltage ratio. (c) Sum channel power ÷ driver efficiency → DC input power → PSU rating with derating margin. (d) Only then compare bus voltages on string fit, part count, cable and connector current, and PSU availability.

**String fit with a buck driver.** Usable string voltage ≈ 0.85 × V_bus (PSU −3 % tolerance, Vf +10 % cold/bin spread, ~2 V dropout) [inference]. With illustrative Vf values (real classes are a later input):

| Bus | Usable | White 3.0 V | Red 2.1 V | Blue 3.2 V | Green 3.3 V |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 24 V | ~20 V | 6 | 9 | 6 | 6 |
| 36 V | ~30 V | 10 | 14 | 9 | 9 |
| 48 V | ~41 V | 13 | 19 | 12 | 12 |

A small Core fixture (6–8 whites) fits a single buck string on any bus. A 60 cm WRGB white channel of 12–14 dies needs two parallel strings (two driver stages) at 24 V but one at 48 V. **Driver-stage count, not efficiency, is the main 24 V penalty on the larger fixture** [inference].

**Topology.** Buck: simplest, cheapest, best ripple, needs V_bus > V_string. Boost from 24 V allows long strings but a boosted string can exceed 60 V DC inside the fixture and leave ES1 territory, a safety-classification cost. Buck-boost is needed only if string voltage straddles bus voltage; avoid by design. At 48 V the fixture must tolerate the adapter's OVP band: Mean Well GST60A48 OVP trips at 50.4–64.8 V [datasheet], so 48 V-bus inputs need ≥ 80 V-class parts, while the 24 V unit trips at 25.2–32.4 V and 40 V-class parts suffice. Buck efficiency is best at high duty: short strings from 48 V run at ~26 % duty with more switching loss; long strings favour 48 V. Both sit in the 90–95 % class; the difference is second-order.

**Cable and connector current** (fixture DC input, illustrative, before driver loss):

| Fixture | 24 V | 36 V | 48 V |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 W | 0.83 A | 0.56 A | 0.42 A |
| 60 W | 2.5 A | 1.67 A | 1.25 A |

Cable loss over a 2 m two-conductor run (22 AWG ≈ 53 mΩ/m, 20 AWG ≈ 33 mΩ/m): 60 W at 24 V on 22 AWG loses 1.3 W (0.53 V); at 48 V 0.33 W. Cable loss is ≤ 2 % in every case and does not decide the voltage. Mean Well fits 18 AWG (GST60A) and 16 AWG (GST90A) to its captive cables [datasheet].

**Connector ratings [datasheet / mfr claim].** DC barrel jacks are mostly voltage-rated at 24 V DC (Kycon KLDX-0202 3.5 A / 24 V; CUI PJ-002A 2.5 A / 24 V): a 48 V bus on a 5.5 mm jack exceeds many jacks' rating even though Mean Well ships 48 V units with the same plug. Weipu SP13: 2-pin 13 A, 4–5-pin 5 A, IP68 mated, gold plated. GX12/GX16 aviation ~5 A, not ingress-rated. M8 ≈ 3–4 A, M12 A-coded ≈ 4 A, IP67 (exact parts RESEARCH REQUIRED). XT30 has no sealed panel-mount version.

**Safety limits [standard clause, as reported].** IEC 62368-1 ES1 ≤ 60 V DC steady state; SELV (IEC 61140) is a different concept and not always equivalent. Both 24 and 48 V nominal are ES1; a 48 V adapter under single fault may briefly present its OVP ceiling (64.8 V), which ES1 permits under single fault but the fixture must survive. Creepage for ≤ 60 V functional insulation is sub-millimetre at pollution degree 2; the real concern near water is pollution degree (condensation, salt film) and electrolytic migration on DC pins, which scales with voltage for a given film resistance: **48 V drives twice the leakage and corrosion current of 24 V** [inference]. Ripple (GST 150–200 mVpp; OWA-60E 200–250 mVpp) is irrelevant to a CC buck stage.

### C3. PSU family standardisation, fixture protection, and sharing with `LT-A`

**"One family, one voltage, one connector, multiple ratings"** is realistic for voltage and safety family, **not automatically for the connector**: within GST the plug is P1J (5.5×2.1) at 60 W and P1M (5.5×2.5) at 90/120 W and the cable gauge changes; a uniform custom plug is possible at MOQ (cost RESEARCH REQUIRED). OWA is IP67 at the body only.

Pitfalls and mitigations [inference]: barrel polarity is convention, not standard (centre-positive on Mean Well), so the fixture must carry reverse-polarity protection (ideal-diode MOSFET preferred). An undersized adapter hiccups at 105–150 % overload [datasheet], a hardware backstop, but the user sees flicker and restarts; fixture-side hardware under-voltage lockout with hysteresis (a comparator, not firmware, per platform §4) plus a firmware power cap after detection is the layered answer. Wrong voltage: 12 V into a 24 V fixture → UVLO holds off; 48 V into 24 V → eFuse overvoltage cut-off + TVS; 24 V into 48 V → UVLO. Adapter identification is impossible on a 2-pin barrel; a third pin (SP13-3/4, M8-3) enables an ID resistor or 1-wire at the cost of leaving the barrel ecosystem, NEEDS DECISION later. A PTC or eFuse in the fixture protects cable and jack against fixture faults.

**Fixture input protection and safety near water.** Reverse polarity (ideal diode), overvoltage (TVS + eFuse), inrush (eFuse dV/dt or NTC), surge (adapter meets IEC 61000-4-5 1 kV / 2 kV on mains [datasheet]; add DC-side TVS since pump and heater switching on the same strip couples EFT through the adapter), ESD IEC 61000-4-2 4 kV / 8 kV at the connector. A Class I adapter (GST) leaves the DC output floating with 0.75–1 mA leakage; a Class II (OWA) is earth-free with < 0.25 mA. In either case Y-capacitor leakage can find a path through fixture metalwork → aquarium water → earth, so a **touch/leakage-current test with the fixture over conductive water** (IEC 62368-1 / IEC 60990 method) belongs in validation. Connector: IP per IEC 60529, salt mist IEC 60068-2-11/-52, damp heat IEC 60068-2-30/-78, mating cycles, and a DC-biased condensation dendrite test.

**Sharing with `LT-A` (48 V remote drivers).** Genuinely shareable at 48 V: LED string designs, driver-stage ICs and layout, firmware dimming model, Vf-class binning rules. Not shareable: the PSU (rack uses enclosed installation supplies in an IP65 enclosure under the RCBO/SPD schedule; the aquarium needs a consumer-class certified adapter under a different BIS route), the connector (IP65 keyed industrial vs consumer), volume and cost class. Mainstream BIS-marked adapters exist at both 24 and 48 V; 24 V has the deeper Indian OEM pool and cheaper 40 V-class fixture parts; 48 V halves cable current and driver count on the larger fixture at the price of 80 V-class parts and 24 V-rated barrel jacks; 36 V is weakest on adapter availability. The commonality benefit with `LT-A` is real but confined to the LED/driver engine; treating it as a PSU or connector economy would be a false economy.

**Later phases must decide** LED Vf classes and dies per channel per size class, channel count, maximum fixture power per size class, adapter cable length and who supplies it, connector IP class and pin count (ID pin or not), Class I vs Class II adapter, BIS category for adapter and fixture, and only then the bus voltage. **Validate**: driver efficiency vs Vin at min/max string; thermal at 40 °C with adapter derating curves; system EMC pre-scan (Mean Well disclaims system-level EMC [datasheet note]); surge/EFT with a pump on the same strip; ESD at the connector; touch current over water; connector ingress, salt mist and mating cycles; wrong-adapter and undersized-adapter fault tests that must fail safe without firmware.

## Part D — Biology summaries (2026-09-11)

Researched 2026-09-11 by the Plant Science specialist. Peer-reviewed sources are
listed at the end of each section. These summaries say what biology supports;
they do not decide what Trophic builds.

### D1. Microgreens (`LT-A` context)

**Headline [inference, high confidence for yield and morphology, medium for quality].** A fixed broad-spectrum white-based light with added red, programmable intensity and programmable photoperiod is sufficient for commercial microgreens. The case for a tunable bar rests on quality-only effects (anthocyanins, carotenoids, glucosinolates) that are species-specific, sometimes contradictory, and not shown to change yield or commercial value.

**Intensity dominates spectrum for yield and compactness [peer-reviewed, consistent across groups].** Gerovac et al. 2016 (kohlrabi, mizuna, mustard at 105/210/315 µmol/m²/s, three spectra): hypocotyl length fell with intensity, fresh weight rose, spectrum effects on fresh weight were small (far-red +8–10 %, green-containing +14–18 % in mustard only), no photodamage at 315. Jones-Baumgardt et al. 2019 (kale, cabbage, arugula, mustard, 100–600): dry weight rose monotonically to 600, no plateau. Samuolienė et al. 2013: 110 suppressed growth, 330–440 optimal, 545 no further benefit.

**Blue fraction [peer-reviewed].** Ying et al. 2020: 5–30 % blue; hypocotyl and cotyledon area of kale and mustard decreased linearly with blue; arugula and cabbage unaffected; yield unaffected except cabbage (peak at 15 %). Brazaitytė et al. 2021: more blue → shorter, more minerals; biomass species-dependent. Samuolienė et al. 2017: carotenoids peak at 33 % blue, tocopherols at 16 %. Caution (Johnson, Kong & Zheng 2019): at 50–100 µmol pure blue *promoted* elongation in arugula; blue is not a universal compactness lever.

**Pre-harvest blue finishing (Kopsell & Sams 2013)** raised carotenoids, glucosinolates and minerals in broccoli microgreens but the treatment also dropped intensity ~88 %, so the effect cannot be attributed to blue alone [peer-reviewed, confounded].

**Green [peer-reviewed].** Alrifai et al. 2022: ~19 % green in an R+B background was the best condition for most species for yield plus phytonutrients. Green is inherent in a white-based spectrum.

**Far-red [peer-reviewed].** Promotes hypocotyl elongation and modestly increases biomass (Gerovac 2016; Shahkoomahally 2025). Longer hypocotyls are often *wanted* for harvest height; "compactness" is not a universal goal.

**Blue-enriched spectrum, no yield gain [peer-reviewed].** Cress under blue-enriched, far-red-reduced LED: anthocyanin +77 %, phenolic index +52 %, growth and productivity unchanged (Frontiers Plant Sci 2026). This is the study the 2026-08 internal review cited for tunability; it supports quality steering, not yield.

**PPFD and DLI.** 150–210 µmol/m²/s at 16 h is DLI 8.6–12.1 mol/m²/day; at 24 h, 13.0–18.1 [arithmetic]. The Brassica evidence range is ~100–400; 150–210 sits lower-middle. Frontiers Sust. Food Syst. 2024 (cabbage, kale, mizuna, mustard at 120/160/210, broad spectrum) found low/medium intensity gave equal or higher fresh weight, 210 higher dry matter and phenolics, and recommended 110–160 on energy grounds. **Some species want less**: broccoli fresh weight peaked at 50–100 (Shahkoomahally 2025); cabbage and Chinese kale optima 70–90 (Liu et al. 2022); pea shoots 35–100 [weaker source]. So the fixture's dim range should reach ≤ 75 µmol/m²/s without flicker; pea and sunflower NEED VALIDATION at Ooty. **Photoinhibition at ≤ 210: no evidence found.** Brassica microgreens were grown to 315, 545 and 600 without visual damage. The inherited rule "above 210 risks photoinhibition" (CEA suite `safety-rules.json`, sourced to Phase A) is therefore a Trophic assumption, not a finding; 210 is defensible as an energy, thermal and uniformity optimum, not a biological limit. Uniformity: pea trials found no yield difference between trays of equal mean PPFD and different uniformity (Horticulturae 2023), so CV ≤ 15 % is comfortably adequate biologically.

**Fixed vs tunable, recommendation: fixed [inference].** No study shows a commercial benefit from changing spectrum mid-cycle; quality effects of blue are real but inconsistent in direction; a fixed 15–25 % blue captures most of the benefit; the strongest tunable use case is confounded with an intensity drop a dimmer reproduces. Tunability is justified only for an R&D fixture at Ooty, a future nutrition-labelled premium crop, or rooms mixing microgreens with flowering crops. None applies to Gen-1.

**Design space for a fixed spectrum (ranges, not a spec):**

| Parameter | Range | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Base | Broad phosphor white so green is present and CRI is high enough for crop inspection | peer-reviewed (Alrifai 2022; Gerovac 2016) |
| CCT | ~3500–5700 K | **assumption**; no peer-reviewed CCT comparison for microgreens found, NEEDS VALIDATION |
| Added ~660 nm red | Red (600–700) at ~40–60 % of PPFD | inference from R:B studies; most efficient photon source |
| Blue (400–500) | 15–25 % of PPFD | peer-reviewed (Ying 2020, Brazaitytė 2021, Samuolienė 2017) |
| Green (500–600) | 15–30 % | peer-reviewed (Alrifai 2022; Frontiers 2024) |
| Far-red | 0–7 %, optional | peer-reviewed +8–10 % FW with elongation; decide by trial |
| UV-A | none in Gen-1 | quality-only effects, no commercial return |

**Photoperiod [peer-reviewed].** 24 h vs 16 h at fixed 270 µmol raised fresh and dry weight in arugula, broccoli, mizuna and radish with a mild stress signature (PMC 8781578). At equal DLI, 24 h still raised yield and lowered nitrate vs 16 h (Russian J. Plant Physiol. 2023): same energy, lower peak PPFD, better tier uniformity, a good fit for the 150–210 band. The fixture needs on/off/dim only, with dark truly dark (< ~1 µmol) for 16 h regimes.

**Biological risks.** Etiolation below ~100 µmol or < ~5 % blue; excess blue (> ~30 %) shrinks cotyledons in kale and mustard; tip-burn is not documented in 7–14 day microgreens; heat risk is humidity and mould under trays rather than radiant load; narrow red-blue "blurple" makes damping-off, mould and yellowing hard to see, which a broad white base resolves.

**Ooty validation trial (proportionate).** Radish, mustard or kale, sunflower, pea. LT-A prototype vs current bought-in fixture at equal mapped PPFD and photoperiod, 3 trays each, 2 consecutive cycles, optional 16 h vs 24 h arm at equal DLI. Measure fresh weight per tray, % dry matter, hypocotyl length (10 seedlings), cotyledon area, colour score, days to harvest, disease incidence; skip lab phytochemistry. Success: LT-A ≥ 95 % of reference fresh weight, hypocotyl within ±15 %, no visual defects, no species needing PPFD outside the dimmable range. Anything less: revisit blue and far-red fractions, not channel count.

**Emersed aquatic nursery relevance.** Emersed Anubias, Bucephalandra and many Cryptocoryne are shade plants; tissue-culture labs use ~50 µmol at 12 h (PMC 12232579). A broad white + red bar dimmed to 50–150 µmol is biologically reasonable; the constraint is humidity, not spectrum. No evidence that spectrum tuning improves emersed grow-out or red colouration; RESEARCH REQUIRED before any claim.

### D2. Planted aquaria (`AQ-LT-A`, `AQ-LT-B` context)

**Carbon, not photons, is usually the binding limit [peer-reviewed].** Gas flux in water is ~10⁴-fold slower than in air; the diffusive boundary layer can be ~90 % of the resistance to carbon fixation; about half of 80 freshwater angiosperms tested cannot use bicarbonate. Leaf-scale light saturation is ~200–400 µmol/m²/s but self-shading in dense stands moves it higher and raises the compensation point 3–8× (Pedersen, Colmer & Sand-Jensen 2013; Madsen & Sand-Jensen 1991). Too much light harms: *Myriophyllum* and *Vallisneria* grew best at 30–50 % of full sunlight and worse above (AoB Plants 2022). Hobby substrate-PPFD tiers, 20–40 (shade), 40–90 (typical), 90–150 (demanding reds), 200+ ("overkill") are field observation (2Hr Aquarist) but consistent with CO₂ being the limit. Evidence-backed statement: **20–150 µmol/m²/s at substrate spans low- to high-tech; above that, returns diminish unless CO₂ is injected.** Species-specific saturation for aquarium plants does not exist in the peer-reviewed record, NEEDS VALIDATION.

**Water attenuation at 20–60 cm is second-order [fact + inference].** Pure-water absorption (Pope & Fry 1997): 450 nm 0.0092 m⁻¹, 550 nm 0.0565, 600 nm 0.222, 660 nm 0.41, 700 nm 0.624. Over 0.4–0.5 m: blue and green lose < 5 %, 660 nm ~15–20 %, 700+ nm 25–30 %. Tannins absorb toward the blue (Markager & Vincent 2000), so a tea-coloured tank flips the picture. Neither justifies shaping spectrum "for depth" at aquarium scale; canopy self-shading and glass-wall escape matter more. Indian-tank tannin levels: RESEARCH REQUIRED (a 440 nm absorbance survey is cheap).

**Spectrum and plant response.** Strong aquatic evidence exists only for non-aquarium species: red-enriched light grew *Potamogeton* best (R:B 4:1 to 8:1) but with narrower leaves and pigment loss under excess red (2025); in 94-day mesocosms *Vallisneria* was indifferent to red/blue/white while *Myriophyllum* grew best under red (Aquatic Ecology 2021). **No peer-reviewed spectrum study exists for Rotala, Ludwigia, Alternanthera, Hemianthus, Eleocharis, Cryptocoryne, Anubias, Bucephalandra or aquarium mosses. RESEARCH REQUIRED.** Terrestrial extrapolation (blue → compactness; low R:FR → elongation; high light + UV-A/blue → anthocyanin) is plausible but submergence alters responses and magnitudes are unknown. Red colour in aquarium plants is, on current evidence, driven by **intensity, CO₂ and nutrient status (Fe, N limitation)** far more than by spectrum.

**"Red plants need red light" versus "RGB makes reds pop" [fact].** Two different things. Rendering: a fixture with narrow R/G/B peaks and a gap near 580 nm produces high colour saturation, so red leaves reflect the red peak and green leaves the green peak; the effect is instant and disappears when the light changes. Physiology: the pigment is only there if the plant made anthocyanin over days to weeks. RGB lights *show* reds better; whether they *make* more red is unproven. Trophic copy must not claim the second.

**Algae [peer-reviewed + field].** Phycobilisome cyanobacteria use blue poorly and grow best at 620–660 nm (Luimstra et al. 2018); in lake mesocosms red favoured cyanobacteria, blue favoured green algae, green favoured diatoms; diatoms need blue for high-light acclimation. So a heavily red-weighted Core spectrum plausibly raises blue-green-algae risk at the margin, and a blue-heavy one favours green algae and diatoms; effect size in a planted tank is unknown, NEEDS VALIDATION. Intensity × duration with unstable CO₂ and nutrients is the dominant driver in every source. Green spot with high light/low PO₄, black-beard with fluctuating CO₂, diatoms in new tanks are plausible field observations. **Siesta**: no demonstrated effect on algae. **Ramps**: no biological evidence either way; a fish-welfare and aesthetic feature that reduces effective DLI harmlessly. Neither may be marketed as algae control.

**Photoperiod.** Laboratory macrophyte work uses 12:12 or 14:10 without harm; no peer-reviewed optimum for aquarium tanks. Hobby practice: 5–6 h new tank, 7–8 h low-tech, 8–9 h high-tech. Without CO₂, hours beyond daily carbon depletion add no growth but keep feeding epiphytes; DLI cap matters more than hours. Usable range 6–10 h, default ~8 h, intensity as the primary lever [assumption, NEEDS VALIDATION].

**UV-A / near-UV.** Terrestrial: moderate 365 nm (~10–20 µmol) raises anthocyanin 17–49 % in lettuce and kale, excess (~30 µmol) causes lipid peroxidation. Aquatic: no study; water plus tannins attenuate 365–400 nm strongly, so dose at substrate is small and uncertain. Week Aqua's "RGB+UVA 395 nm for plant growth and fish colour" is a manufacturer claim with no data; fish "tanning" is pigment fluorescence, a rendering effect. Risks: polymer lens and silicone yellowing, tannin photobleaching, viewer discomfort, an over-dose channel. **No evidence-backed biological case for UVA at Phase 0.** If ever included, treat as a dose-limited visual feature.

**Core fixed spectrum: all four classes are biologically adequate** (white-only; warm+cool white; white+red; white+red+blue) for low-to-medium-tech tanks; broad white already carries usable blue and red. Adding 630–660 nm raises photon efficacy and offsets the ~15–20 % red water loss; adding royal blue mainly raises visual saturation with a plausible compactness benefit; avoid a red-dominated engine for the cyanobacteria reason. **There is no biological evidence a user needs spectral control**; it is an aesthetic and rendering feature and a hedge for unknown species responses. A single well-chosen fixed spectrum with excellent intensity control and scheduling loses nothing biologically that can currently be demonstrated [inference, NEEDS VALIDATION by trial].

**WRGB channel biology.** RGB-only leaves a 570–600 nm hole that plants do not need filled but human vision and fish colour fidelity do; a white channel gives the broad baseline the mesocosm evidence supports; a 660 nm channel is the most photon-efficient way to raise PPFD without more blue; a 450 nm channel is where terrestrial compactness/anthocyanin extrapolations would act; green has no demonstrated aquatic physiological role beyond penetration and rendering; far-red is attenuated most and its elongation response is the opposite of what aquascapers want.

**Validation trial (proportionate, Ooty).** Six identical 60 cm tanks: three high-tech (CO₂, EI-type dosing, aquasoil), three low-tech; within each, Core candidate, WRGB candidate at a showroom setting, and a broad-white reference at matched substrate PPFD (quantum sensor underwater), 8 h photoperiod, matched DLI. Plants: *Rotala rotundifolia* 'H'ra' or 'Blood Red', *Ludwigia* 'Super Red', *Alternanthera reineckii* 'Mini', *Hemianthus callitrichoides*, *Eleocharis*, *Cryptocoryne wendtii*, *Anubias nana*, one moss. Weekly for 10–12 weeks, two runs: internode length, stem elongation, end dry mass, leaf colour by a fixed colorimetric protocol under a standard lamp, algae onset by type and day, pH/KH/drop-checker/NO₃/PO₄. Success: Core ≥ reference on growth and colour and ≤ reference on algae; WRGB must show a *measurable* pigment or compactness gain over Core before any biological claim, otherwise WRGB is marketed on rendering and control. Add a 440 nm absorbance survey of ~20 hobbyist tanks.

**Emersed nursery grow-out** is ordinary horticulture: high humidity, free CO₂, tolerates higher PPFD; a leafy-crop spectrum is adequate; the levers are humidity and hardening. Nursery PPFD targets RESEARCH REQUIRED.
