What this document is, and what it is not

This is an engineering validation record, not a certification. Everything in it is traceable to a calculation, a model query or a stated assumption — and the limitations are listed as prominently as the passes.

Validation methods used, and their weight

Closed-form calculation

Beam deflection, hydraulic capacity, pump duty, NIOSH lifting index, heat and moisture balance, electrical loading. Shown with the arithmetic in the source documents. Adequate to commit to geometry.

CAD interference analysis

All 165 occurrences checked pairwise, coincident faces excluded, connected pairs whitelisted explicitly. Proves geometric non-overlap at nominal dimensions only.

Model property query

Envelope, per-part and total mass, occurrence counts, read directly from the model rather than transcribed. Only as good as the modelled wall thicknesses.

Failure-mode enumeration

Each mode given a detection means and a de-energised state. Systematic, but not a quantified FMEA — no occurrence or detection scoring.

What has not been done, and must not be implied. No finite-element analysis: no buckling check on the uprights under eccentric load, no fatigue, no weld or bolted-joint stress. No CFD: airflow is continuity-based only. No tolerance stack-up, so interference-clean at nominal dimensions is not the same as proven assemblable. No LED junction-temperature model. No envelope thermal model against Ooty's actual climate year. Every "PASS" below carries these limits.

T18 is not busywork. A weight check is the cheapest single test that catches a systematic build error — wrong wall thickness on the tube, a solid deck instead of perforated, the wrong tray material. If the first article does not weigh what the model says, something is different, and it is far better to find out on the weighbridge than on the fifth rack.

One honest caveat on the word "approved". Every structural, hydraulic and thermal result in this record is a closed-form hand calculation checked against a CAD model. That is the right level of rigour to commit to geometry and to build a first article. It is not the level of rigour to certify a product for sale. Before the platform is sold as a commercial product, the structural cases should be confirmed by finite-element analysis, the airflow by measurement or CFD, and the electrical installation by an inspection to IS 732 by a licensed contractor.
