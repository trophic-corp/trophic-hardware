## 1. What the product is

Aquascaping hand tools and accessories: scissors, tweezers, substrate spatulas, algae scrapers, and the stands, mats and holders around them.

## 2. Why it exists

The entry product in the range. Low technical risk, low capital, and the way a brand gets into a hobbyist's hands before they buy a filter. It is also the only product here Trophic could plausibly ship first.

## 3. Inherited constraints — DECIDED

**None.** No Trophic decision has been made about this product. It inherits the
identifier standard and, where it is an electronic device, the principle that a
device must be safe with no power and no signal. Nothing else.

## 4. Industry reference points — NOT TROPHIC DECISIONS

Typical market values, to bound the design space. **Not specifications. No drawing may
cite this section.**

| Item | Typical range | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Material | Stainless steel, commonly 304 or 420-series | 420 takes an edge; 304 resists corrosion better |
| Finish | Polished, matte, or black oxide | Aesthetic, and it is most of the perceived value |
| Lengths | Roughly 20–35 cm for tools reaching into a planted tank | Tank depth dependent |
| Typical set | Straight scissors, curved scissors, tweezers, spatula | The conventional starter set |

## 6. Explicitly not decided

Nothing. Tool geometry is a design exercise nobody has started.

Note that this is the one product where **industrial design matters more than
engineering**, and where a CAD model would be the design rather than a
representation of one.

No dimensions, no materials, no BOM, no cost, no performance figures of any kind.

## 7. Interfaces

Platform **mechanical** standards do not apply — they are written for fabricated steel
structural products. Platform **electrical** standards apply only in principle
(fail-safe, connector discipline); the CEA mains protection schedule does not.
